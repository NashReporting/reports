The five things worth knowing

  • Hyperliquid is an outlier business. ~11 people, no VC, ~$1B+ in annual fees, ~$106M revenue per employee — reportedly the highest in the world, ahead of Tether, Nvidia and Apple.
  • ~99% of fees buy back HYPE. The protocol routes nearly all fees into an "Assistance Fund" that buys back and removes HYPE from supply — >$1.3B spent to date. The team takes essentially none of it.
  • Trade[XYZ] landed a real S&P deal. On Mar 18 2026, S&P Dow Jones Indices officially licensed the S&P 500 to Trade[XYZ] — the first officially licensed on-chain S&P 500 perpetual. It now drives ~90% of HIP-3 open interest.
  • Ventuals is a brother-sister startup. Alvin & Emily Hsia, both ex-Airbnb engineers and Paradigm EIRs, run a ~4–5 person team out of Paradigm's SoHo office trading perps on SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic, etc.
  • The model has real risk. On May 28 2026, Ventuals' SpaceX perp crashed ~45% in 30 minutes after an oracle mishandled a stock split, liquidating ~484 users (~$1.7M); Ventuals pledged compensation. Pre-IPO perps rely on opaque single-oracle feeds.

Hyperliquid

A sovereign Layer-1 blockchain whose flagship app is a fully on-chain perpetual-futures exchange. Custom BFT consensus (HyperBFT) runs the order book on-chain — closer to a CEX in performance, but decentralized.

What it is

Two execution layers: HyperCore (native high-performance on-chain order book / perps engine) and HyperEVM (Ethereum-compatible smart-contract layer, live Feb 2025, secured by the same consensus). Native token HYPE is used for staking and gas.

Entities

  • Hyper Foundation — founded 2024 to support ecosystem/governance (hyperfoundation.org).

Location & size

  • ~11 people, roughly half engineers; headcount grows slowly.
  • Jurisdiction of incorporation uncertain — Cayman Islands reported but not primary-verified.

The money model — confirmed: no VC

  • Zero outside funding. Jeff Yan declined a ~$1B valuation term sheet (Jan 2024) and reportedly a later ~$10B offer.
  • Self-funded: Yan personally bankrolled operations ("hundreds of thousands per month") from Chameleon Trading profits.
  • HYPE airdrop (Nov 29 2024): 310M HYPE = 31% of 1B supply to ~94,000 users — one of the largest airdrops ever (~$14B at ~$45). No VC pre-allocation; ~76% of supply earmarked for community.
  • Revenue → buybacks: ~99% of perp & spot fees auto-convert to HYPE via the Assistance Fund (1% to HLP vault since Aug 30 2025). "Not a single penny went to the team."
Founder framing: "Hyperliquid isn't a company. It's a protocol, and neutrality has been a priority from day one." — Jeff Yan. Reported ~$900M profit in 2025.

Trade[XYZ] (trade.xyz)

The leading HIP-3 builder on Hyperliquid — a perpetual-futures interface for traditional, real-world assets: stock indices, single stocks, commodities, FX, and pre-IPO names. Famous for securing an official S&P 500 license.

Entity confirmation: This is the crypto builder at app.trade.xyz, confirmed via the official S&P press release. Not to be confused with the unrelated UK "TRADE XYZ LTD" (Companies House #14731329), Japan's "XYZ Inc," Trader Joe, trader.xyz (NFTs), or XYZ Laboratory.

What it offers

Markets

  • Equity indices: S&P 500 (licensed), Nasdaq/XYZ100
  • Single stocks: TSLA, NVDA, AAPL, MSFT, AMZN, META, GOOGL, PLTR, COIN, MSTR + pre-market
  • Commodities: gold, silver, platinum, palladium, crude oil, nat gas, copper
  • FX & regional: EUR, JPY; Korea & Japan equities
  • Pre-IPO: SpaceX (SPCX) live May 18 2026; OpenAI/Anthropic planned

Mechanics & scale

  • Leverage typically 3x–50x; settled in USDC (some USDH)
  • ~90% of all HIP-3 open interest
  • Of Hyperliquid's top 30 markets, only ~7 are crypto — the rest are trade.xyz synthetics
  • "Hyperliquid as the new Nasdaq" — Bloomberg reportedly cited its crude-oil price as a reference

Ventuals

An on-chain derivatives protocol that lets anyone trade perpetual futures on the valuations of private, pre-IPO companies (SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic, Stripe, Anduril) plus thematic indices. Built on Hyperliquid — "the first production application of the HIP-3 standard."

Contracts confer zero ownership — no equity, dividends, or voting. Traders speculate purely on valuation movement. Consumer-friendly onboarding via Privy email/social login wallets.

How it works

Contract unitValuation ÷ 1 billion = price (a $420.69B company trades at ~$420.69)
SettlementCash-settled in USDH stablecoin (deployer-dependent)
OracleHybrid: docs say ⅓ off-chain "Notice" data + ⅔ 2-hour EMA of mark price (earlier testnet: 50/50, 8h EMA). Off-chain data from Notice.co: secondary trades, funding rounds, mutual-fund marks, 409A valuations, comparables
Price bandMark must stay within ±20% of oracle
LeverageUp to 20x (docs) — secondary/testnet say 10x; likely raised over time
IPO settlementOn the company's IPO, perp resolves at first-day market close valuation
Source conflicts flagged: leverage (10x vs 20x) and oracle weighting (50/50 vs 33/67) differ between testnet and current docs. Two early outlets describe an "optimistic oracle" (stake-and-challenge) — this contradicts the official docs and is likely outdated/inaccurate.

⚠️ The SpaceX crash (May 28 2026)

The SPACEX-USDH perp plunged ~45% in ~30 minutes (≈$2,277 → $1,254, partial recovery) after the Notice.co oracle mishandled a SpaceX 5-for-1 stock split, cascading liquidations. ~484 users liquidated, ~$1.74M wiped out. Ventuals pledged to compensate within 48 hours. This is the clearest evidence of the structural risk: pre-IPO perps depend on single, opaque oracle feeds with no public reference price.

Regulatory posture

  • Structured via a Panama entity; geoblocks restricted jurisdictions (Hyperliquid itself blocks US residents)
  • Deliberately framed as "valuation-linked perpetual futures, not securities"; consulted Paradigm's regulatory group + external counsel
  • Inherent legal sensitivity — pre-IPO valuation perps could show securities characteristics (ambiguous SEC/CFTC territory)
  • Validation signal: OKX, Crypto.com, Bitget have since launched their own pre-IPO perps — the category is going mainstream

The Builder Ecosystem

Hyperliquid's real innovation is turning an exchange into a permissionless market-creation platform. Third parties can list tokens, build front-ends, and deploy their own perpetual markets — and they pay Hyperliquid for the privilege.

The enabling stack (HIP standards)

StandardEnablesKey terms
HIP-1Native token standard — deploying a token auto-creates a USDC spot order bookTicker via Dutch auction (paid in HYPE since May 2025)
HIP-2"Hyperliquidity" — automated on-chain two-sided liquidity, ~0.3% spreadBuilt into block logic; no operators
HIP-3Builder-deployed perpetuals — anyone can launch a custom perp DEX500k HYPE stake (≥183 days); deployer fee share 0–300%; slashing risk

HIP-3 launched Oct 13 2025 and by March 2026 was >35% of all Hyperliquid volume and >$25B cumulative. HIP-4 (prediction markets) and a controversial HIP-5 (second assistance fund for ecosystem tokens) are proposed/evolving. provisional

How builders make money — and why Hyperliquid wins

Builder codes (front-ends)

  • Capture a fee on routed fills — max 0.1% perps / 1% spot
  • >$40M paid to developers since launch
  • ~40% of daily active users now trade via third-party front-ends

HIP-3 deployers (markets)

  • Set a 0–300% fee share on markets they create
  • In exchange for staking 500k HYPE + slashing risk
  • Brings new asset classes (equities, RWAs, pre-IPO) to the chain
The flywheel: third parties bring distribution and new markets; Hyperliquid keeps the protocol fee, drives HYPE demand (auctions/stakes paid in HYPE, fees → buybacks), and deepens liquidity. It's a "fee-sharing marketing model" that outsources growth — there is no large first-party grant fund; support is structural, not discretionary.

Competitive landscape

  • Hyperliquid leads: ~44% of perp-DEX volume (up from 36.4% in Jan 2026), >70% of decentralized perp open interest
  • The 2025 scare: Aster briefly hit ~70% of volume (Sept 2025) while Hyperliquid dipped to ~10% — Hyperliquid reclaimed the top by Jan 2026
  • Field: edgeX (~26.6%), Aster (~21%), then Jupiter, dYdX, GMX, Drift each <3%
  • Differentiator: the permissionless RWA/equity layer (HIP-3) that competitors largely lack

Caveats & Sources

Read before quoting. This report draws on primary sources where possible (official docs, the S&P press release, founder LinkedIn/X) and reputable secondary outlets elsewhere. Crypto reporting is noisy — the items below are where sources genuinely disagree or where data is undisclosed.

Key uncertainties

  • Incorporation jurisdictions of Hyperliquid's entities are not primary-verified (Cayman reported). Trade[XYZ]'s parent entity/HQ/headcount are entirely undisclosed. Ventuals' "VNTL Markets S.A. (Panama)" comes from a terms-page extraction — verify on the live page.
  • Hyperliquid figures vary by definition: "revenue" ≈ "fees" ≈ "buyback spend" (~99% overlap) → $0.7B–$1.3B annualized depending on source. Market share 31.9%/44%/70% uses different denominators. TVL $1.75B (DefiLlama) vs $2.8–6.2B (bundled) elsewhere.
  • Ventuals mechanics differ between testnet and docs: leverage 10x vs 20x; oracle 50/50 vs 33/67. The "optimistic oracle" description in two outlets contradicts official docs.
  • Founding/mainnet dates for Hyperliquid vary (2021 vs 2022 start; Q1 vs June 2023 mainnet). The trade.xyz "Oct 2024" launch date in one outlet is almost certainly a typo for Oct 2025.
  • Single-source claims to treat cautiously: Goldman buying HYPE / Bitwise BHYP staking; the Hsias' universities; Yan's master's degree and internships.
  • SpaceX pre-IPO attribution: both Trade[XYZ] (SPCX) and Ventuals (SPACEX-USDH) ran SpaceX perps — some outlets cross-attribute. They appear to be distinct deployers/contracts.

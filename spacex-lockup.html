Confidential Analysis

SpaceX IPO — Lock-Up & Liquidity Provisions

Analysis of resale restrictions on existing private holders, drawn from the primary filing

Issuer: Space Exploration Technologies Corp. Ticker: SPCX (Nasdaq / Nasdaq Texas) Source: Form S-1/A (Amendment No. 1) Prepared: June 5, 2026
Bottom line

The filing creates three distinct classes of holder with very different liquidity. A 180-day tier (most existing stockholders) with a heavily front-loaded early-release schedule; a 366-day tier (the Founder + certain significant investors) with no early release at all; and a Directed Share Program (select employees / friends & family) with no lock-up whatsoever. Your liquidity depends entirely on which class your shares sit in.

1. The Directed Share Program — the no-lock-up carve-out

In the Underwriting section, SpaceX reserves a tranche of the offering for insiders that is exempt from any resale restriction. Per the filing:

"the underwriters have reserved up to [ ] percent of the shares… through a directed share program to certain employees and persons identified by our management, which may include parties with whom we have a business relationship and friends and family of management. If purchased by these persons, these shares will not be subject to a lock-up restriction."

These holders can sell as soon as the stock begins trading, subject only to the company's insider-trading policy and to securities-law restrictions for anyone holding material non-public information. Press reporting puts the reserved amount at ~5% of the offering; the exact percentage is left blank in this preliminary prospectus.

2. Two lock-up tiers for everyone else

Unlike a conventional single 180-day lock-up, the S-1/A defines two separate tiers.

Tier 1 180-day lock-up

The company, all directors, and executive officers agree not to sell for 180 days from the date of the prospectus — but with the staggered early-release schedule in Section 3 below. This is the bucket where a large share of holdings can be sold well before the 180-day mark.

Tier 2 366-day lock-up — Founder & significant investors

A separate, stricter tier captures the Founder and a defined set of large holders:

"Our Founder and certain significant investors have agreed… that during a period of 366 days after the date of this prospectus, an aggregate of [ ] shares owned by them (including 100% of the shares owned by our Founder)… This total amount of shares will not be subject to any early release provisions."
Worth noting

This corrects a common misreading in press coverage. Elon Musk is not locked for 180 days — his entire holding is locked for a full 366 days with zero early release, and an unspecified group of "significant investors" is bound to that same year-long tier. If an existing private investor is deemed "significant," they may sit here rather than in the 180-day tier.

3. The Tier 1 early-release schedule

For shares designated "Early Release Eligible Shares," portions are released automatically before the 180-day expiry, measured from the offering date:

TriggerShares released
After Q2 2026 earnings ("First Earnings Release Date," quarter ended June 30, 2026)up to 20%
If closing price is ≥30% above IPO price for ≥5 of the 10 consecutive trading days ending on the First Earnings Release Date+10%
On each of days 70, 90, 105, 120, and 135 after the offering (7% per date)+35%
After Q3 2026 earnings (quarter ended Sept 30, 2026)+28%
180 days after the offeringremainder

The schedule is deliberately front-loaded so the market does not face a single large supply event at day 180, and so the public float (and index eligibility) builds gradually.

4. Two provisions that change the picture

  • M&A carve-out. The lock-up "will not apply to securities to be issued by the Company in connection with any mergers, acquisitions or similar transactions during the lock-up period." The filing adds that "certain additional exceptions… will be detailed in a subsequent amendment." This is the same equity-issuance language driving market speculation about a large strategic transaction.
  • Discretionary waiver. Goldman Sachs & Co. LLC, as representative of the underwriters, may release any party from these lock-ups at any time with prior written consent.

5. What this means for a current private holder

  • Tier 1 General existing stockholder → realistic first liquidity is the Q2 earnings release (20%), then the day-70+ tranches — not IPO day.
  • Tier 2 "Significant investor" → potentially locked a full 366 days with no staggered exits, alongside the Founder. A materially worse liquidity profile; confirm directly whether your holding is so designated.
  • DSP Directed Share Program → no lock-up, but this is reserved for employees / friends & family and is unlikely to apply to outside private investors.
Action

The single most important thing to confirm with your fund / SPV manager is which tier your specific shares were placed in (180-day vs. 366-day), and whether your SPV layers any additional restriction on top of the issuer lock-up.

6. Who are the "certain significant investors"? — inference, not filing-confirmed

The filing does not name which holders are bound to the 366-day tier — the share count is one of the blanks. The list below is reasoned inference, anchored to what the S-1 does disclose (its beneficial-ownership table and director affiliations) plus public knowledge of SpaceX's cap table.

What the S-1 actually names

The only holders disclosed above the 5% threshold are the Founder and one outside group:

VC-affiliated directors are named but each holds <1% personally: Randy Glein (Founders Fund), Luke Nosek (Gigafund / Founders Fund), Steve Jurvetson (DFJ / Future Ventures), Ira Ehrenpreis (DBL Partners), Donald Harrison (Google/Alphabet). The names "Sequoia" and "Andreessen" do not appear anywhere in the filing, and no round leads are disclosed — financing detail is limited to the EchoStar spectrum purchase and debt.

Most likely "significant investors" (ranked by confidence)

  • 1. Valor Equity Partners (Gracias) — highest confidence. The only outside holder above 5% (7.3%); longtime board member and close Musk ally. A block this size is exactly what an issuer locks for a full year.
  • 2. Founders Fund / Gigafund — the earliest, most Musk-aligned backers (Glein and Nosek on the board); natural long-horizon holders.
  • 3. Google / Alphabet — large 2015 strategic investor with a board seat (Harrison); strategic stakes are typically locked longer.
  • 4. Sequoia Capital and Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) — among the largest later-round investors publicly reported to hold meaningful SpaceX stakes (not named in the filing).
  • 5. Fidelity, Baillie Gifford, large sovereign / crossover funds — lower confidence; crossover money more often negotiates the standard 180-day lock and may sit in Tier 1.
Read this as inference

"Significant investors" is defined by the underwriters' lock-up agreements, not the 5% ownership threshold — so the real list could be broader (a negotiated set) or narrower (Valor plus one or two funds). Only Musk and Valor are filing-confirmed as large blocks; items 2–5 are informed inference from public sources.

7. Important caveat — preliminary figures

This is a preliminary S-1/A. The early-release mechanics and triggers above are in final-form language, but the underlying quantities are left blank in the filing: the share counts in each tier, the percentage of shares allocated to the 366-day tier, and the Directed Share Program percentage. These will not be fixed until a later amendment / the final pricing prospectus. Treat all percentages tied to specific share counts as not-yet-final.
